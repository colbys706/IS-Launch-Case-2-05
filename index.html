    EXPLORE A CAREER · 01

    IT Project Manager

    Bring people, plans, and technology together. Guide a project from its first idea to its final delivery.

    What the role does day to day

    • Plan schedules, milestones, budgets, tasks, and project resources.
    • Run meetings, communicate updates, assign or coordinate work, and follow up on blockers.
    • Work with technical teams and business stakeholders to clarify requirements and priorities.
    • Track risks, issues, dependencies, scope changes, and deadlines.
    • Prepare status reports and help the team deliver on time and within budget.

    Technical skills and tools

    • Project planning and scheduling
    • Requirements and stakeholder communication
    • Risk and issue management
    • Agile/Scrum fundamentals
    • Excel/Google Sheets
    • Jira, Azure DevOps, Asana, Monday.com, or similar tools
    • Presentation and documentation skills

    Before your first internship

    What an entry-level or internship candidate should know:

    • Understand basic project-management concepts such as scope, schedule, milestones, dependencies, risks, and stakeholders.
    • Be comfortable with Excel/Sheets and presentation/documentation tools.
    • Know basic Agile/Scrum vocabulary and how a project team works.
    • Be able to create a simple project plan, task tracker, status update, or risk log.
    • Internships often value communication, organization, leadership in group projects, and evidence that you can follow through.

    Salary and career progression

    Estimated U.S. annual salary ranges

    Early career$60K–$85K
    Experienced$90K–$130K+

    These ranges are estimates—not exact ranges published by the sources. Actual pay depends on the job title, location, industry, employer, platform or specialty, and experience.

    A possible career path

    1. Project Coordinator / Associate
    2. Project Manager
    3. Senior Project Manager
    4. Program / Portfolio Manager or PMO leadership

    Industry, location, and responsibility affect pay and progression.

    Could this be your fit?

    Best fit for students who enjoy organizing people and work, communicating frequently, solving problems, and keeping a project moving toward a deadline.

    What makes a strong candidate?

    • Highly organized and dependable
    • Clear communicator
    • Comfortable coordinating different personalities and priorities
    • Proactive about problems rather than waiting for them to become emergencies
    • Able to balance business goals with technical constraints
    • Demonstrates leadership even without a formal leadership title

    EXPLORE A CAREER · 02

    Data Analytics

    Find the story in the numbers. Turn raw data into insights that help teams make better decisions.

    What the role does day to day

    • Collect, clean, organize, and validate data.
    • Write queries and analyze datasets to answer business questions.
    • Build dashboards, reports, charts, and visualizations.
    • Look for trends, anomalies, relationships, and performance indicators.
    • Explain findings to nontechnical stakeholders and help teams make data-informed decisions.

    Technical skills and tools

    • SQL
    • Excel/Google Sheets
    • Python basics (often helpful)
    • Data cleaning and transformation
    • Statistics fundamentals
    • Tableau, Power BI, or similar BI tools
    • Data visualization
    • Business communication and storytelling

    Before your first internship

    What an entry-level or internship candidate should know:

    • Be comfortable with SQL basics: SELECT, filtering, joins, grouping, and aggregations.
    • Know spreadsheet fundamentals such as formulas, pivot tables, and charts.
    • Understand basic descriptive statistics and how to interpret a dataset.
    • Be able to create a simple dashboard or analytical report.
    • A portfolio project that starts with a business question and ends with a clear recommendation is especially useful.

    Salary and career progression

    Estimated U.S. annual salary ranges

    Early career$55K–$80K
    Experienced$80K–$120K+

    These ranges are estimates—not exact ranges published by the sources. Actual pay depends on the job title, location, industry, employer, platform or specialty, and experience.

    A possible career path

    1. Data Analyst
    2. Senior Data Analyst
    3. Analytics Lead / Manager
    4. Analytics Manager / Director

    Related paths include business intelligence, product analytics, data engineering, and data science. Specialized analytics roles can pay more.

    Could this be your fit?

    Best fit for students who like numbers, patterns, evidence, visualization, and turning messy information into useful business decisions.

    What makes a strong candidate?

    • Curious and analytical
    • Comfortable asking 'what does the data tell us?'
    • Strong attention to detail
    • Can explain technical findings in plain language
    • Builds dashboards/projects rather than only listing coursework
    • Understands that business context matters as much as the numbers

    EXPLORE A CAREER · 03

    Cyber Security Analyst

    Help protect systems and information. Investigate risks, spot threats, and strengthen defenses.

    What the role does day to day

    • Monitor networks, systems, alerts, and security tools for suspicious activity.
    • Investigate potential security incidents and determine what happened.
    • Review vulnerabilities, logs, access controls, and security configurations.
    • Help implement security controls, policies, and remediation steps.
    • Document incidents and communicate findings to technical and business teams.
    • Depending on the organization, participate in threat detection, vulnerability management, identity/access management, or incident response.

    Technical skills and tools

    • Networking fundamentals
    • Operating systems basics
    • Security principles and common threats
    • Linux/Windows fundamentals
    • Python or scripting basics
    • SQL/log querying can help
    • SIEM/security tools
    • Identity and access concepts
    • Cloud-security fundamentals
    • Security frameworks and documentation

    Before your first internship

    What an entry-level or internship candidate should know:

    • Understand networking concepts such as IP addresses, DNS, HTTP/S, ports, and common network devices.
    • Know basic security concepts including authentication, authorization, encryption, vulnerabilities, phishing, malware, and least privilege.
    • Be comfortable navigating Windows and/or Linux.
    • Be able to investigate a basic alert or explain how you would troubleshoot a security problem.
    • Hands-on labs, home labs, CTFs, security projects, internships, or entry-level IT experience can strengthen an application.

    Salary and career progression

    Estimated U.S. annual salary ranges

    Early career$65K–$90K
    Experienced$90K–$140K+

    These ranges are estimates—not exact ranges published by the sources. Actual pay depends on the job title, location, industry, employer, platform or specialty, and experience.

    A possible career path

    1. Security / SOC Analyst
    2. Security Engineer or Senior Analyst
    3. Security Lead / Manager
    4. Security Architect, Incident Response Lead, or Security Director

    Specializations can include threat detection, vulnerability management, identity and access management, and incident response.

    Could this be your fit?

    Best fit for students who enjoy investigating problems, spotting vulnerabilities, thinking about threats, and protecting systems and information.

    What makes a strong candidate?

    • Naturally curious and investigative
    • Patient with troubleshooting and ambiguity
    • Strong attention to detail
    • Understands that security is both technical and organizational
    • Builds hands-on technical experience
    • Can document and communicate an incident clearly

    EXPLORE A CAREER · 04

    ERP Systems Consultant

    Connect business needs with technology. Help organizations make their core systems work together.

    What the role does day to day

    • Meet with business users to understand processes, pain points, and requirements.
    • Translate business needs into ERP system requirements or configurations.
    • Help configure, test, implement, or improve ERP modules and workflows.
    • Map current processes and identify opportunities for automation or improvement.
    • Troubleshoot system/process issues and coordinate with technical teams.
    • Support implementations, user training, documentation, data migration, and change management.

    Technical skills and tools

    • Business-process analysis
    • Requirements gathering
    • ERP concepts
    • SQL/database fundamentals
    • Excel
    • Process mapping
    • Testing and documentation
    • Data/integration concepts
    • Communication and presentation
    • ERP platforms such as SAP, Oracle, Microsoft Dynamics, or similar

    Before your first internship

    What an entry-level or internship candidate should know:

    • Understand core business functions such as finance, supply chain, sales, HR, or operations.
    • Know how business requirements become system requirements.
    • Be comfortable with process mapping, documentation, spreadsheets, and basic data concepts.
    • Understand what an ERP does and why companies use integrated systems.
    • Internship/project experience involving business processes, systems analysis, ERP, or consulting is valuable.

    Salary and career progression

    Estimated U.S. annual salary ranges

    Early career$70K–$95K
    Experienced$95K–$175K

    These ranges are estimates—not exact ranges published by the sources. Actual pay depends on the job title, location, industry, employer, platform or specialty, and experience.

    A possible career path

    1. Analyst / Associate Consultant
    2. Consultant
    3. Senior Consultant
    4. Solution Architect / Manager
    5. Program / Practice leadership

    ERP has no single clean occupational salary benchmark. Specializing in a major platform or business area can strengthen your marketability.

    Could this be your fit?

    Best fit for students who like both business and technology and enjoy understanding how an organization works, then improving the systems and processes that support it.

    What makes a strong candidate?

    • Interested in both business and technology
    • Strong communicator and listener
    • Good at translating between technical and nontechnical people
    • Enjoys understanding how processes work
    • Comfortable learning complex software
    • Can organize requirements, test cases, documentation, and stakeholder feedback

    EXPLORE A CAREER · 05

    Software / Application Developer

    Build tools that people use. Design, write, test, and improve software that solves real problems.

    What the role does day to day

    • Write and maintain application code.
    • Build features based on requirements and user needs.
    • Debug problems, write tests, and fix defects.
    • Work with APIs, databases, version control, and development environments.
    • Review code and collaborate with other developers, designers, product managers, and stakeholders.
    • Deploy, monitor, and improve applications over time.

    Technical skills and tools

    • JavaScript/TypeScript, Java, Python, C#, or another relevant language
    • Git/GitHub
    • HTML/CSS for web roles
    • SQL and databases
    • APIs
    • Testing/debugging
    • Frameworks such as React, Node.js, .NET, Spring, or similar
    • Basic cloud/deployment concepts
    • Problem solving and algorithms

    Before your first internship

    What an entry-level or internship candidate should know:

    • Be able to write working code in at least one language.
    • Understand variables, functions, conditionals, loops, data structures, and basic object-oriented concepts.
    • Know Git basics and be able to work with a repository.
    • Be able to build and explain at least one meaningful project.
    • Internships and entry-level roles often evaluate coding ability through projects, technical interviews, or coding assessments.

    Salary and career progression

    Estimated U.S. annual salary ranges

    Early career$70K–$100K
    Experienced$100K–$160K+

    These ranges are estimates—not exact ranges published by the sources. Actual pay depends on the job title, location, industry, employer, platform or specialty, and experience.

    A possible career path

    1. Junior / Associate Developer
    2. Software Developer
    3. Senior Developer
    4. Staff / Principal Engineer or Engineering Manager

    Developers can specialize in frontend, backend, full-stack, mobile, cloud, or security. Pay varies by specialization, industry, location, and company.

    Could this be your fit?

    Best fit for students who enjoy building things, coding, debugging, learning technical concepts, and turning an idea into a working application.

    What makes a strong candidate?

    • Enjoys building and experimenting
    • Can solve problems independently
    • Has a visible GitHub/project portfolio
    • Can explain technical decisions
    • Persistent when debugging
    • Keeps learning as technologies change
